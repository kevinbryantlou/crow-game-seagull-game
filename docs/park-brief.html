§0 Where this stands

Built and verified. Both harnesses pass: smoke runs every level-design rule against it and shoot drives it in real WebGL, photographs it, and measures the dusk frames. It has not been played by a person yet, so every claim on this page about feel is a hypothesis and every claim about fact is asserted somewhere in the repo.

The reason it exists is a note from the roofline's playtest: the roofline reads as too big a scale leap to follow the block directly. It went to slot 3 and left a hole at slot 2. This is the block that fills it — and the whole design constraint is that it must be a small step, not a good level in isolation.

The ladder, now that there are three rungs $20 on the block · $25 here · $30 on the roofline. One deck · two decks · four decks. The park's job is to be the second number in each of those rows, and nothing more ambitious than that.

§1 What a small step is made of

A level that follows the block has two things it can spend: height and pressure. The roofline spent everything on height — four decks, a nest twelve metres up, money priced entirely by the climb. That is what made it a leap.

So the park spends almost nothing on height and most of what it has on pressure. There is exactly one roof, it is 3.4 m up, and the nest sits 1.35 m above that on the pavilion's vent housing. A crow arriving at the bottom of that climb with a mostly-empty stamina bar clears it; RULES.maxUnbrokenClimb is 9 m and the smoke report puts the tallest unbroken climb to this nest at 1.0 m, because the roof itself is a landing. The y axis is introduced as a fact, not as a problem.

What is harder is who is looking at you. The block gives each district one guard with one pitch to watch, and the answer is always "work the district next door". The park's marquee is one object with three people standing round it, and there is no district next door — the other guarded money is forty metres away at the far end.

No new verbs Fly, take one thing, bank it, shove the weight off a note, bait a guard with food, trade a shiny with a kid. All six are level 1's, taught on level 1. The only new noun is the soft pretzel, which is a hot dog with a different smell — a third member of BAIT_KINDS, and adding it made the audit's bait rule read the vocabulary instead of naming two kinds by hand.

§2 The plan

Five places, laid out so that the two ends are both guarded and the middle is not. The block ran money-rich to money-poor left to right; the park runs safe in the middle to dangerous at both edges, which is a different shape of run and is meant to be.

Four shinies pay a maximum of $9.00 across the kid's ladder ([1.25, 1.75, 2.50, 3.50]). So the honest route — everything you can pick up without crossing anybody, plus every trade she will ever make — tops out at $12.85 of $25. That bound is asserted rather than eyeballed, because a trade ladder is a knob with an obvious failure mode: pay enough and a game about robbing a pretzel vendor becomes a game about finding bottle caps.

The ladder pays more than the block's and less than the roofline's, for the reason it always does — a trade here costs a walk across open grass past a picnic that is watching the grass. Worse odds than an empty plaza corner, far better than carrying a marble up a hotel.

§4 The picnic

Three people stand round a cooler. On the lid, in plain sight, glinting from anywhere in the west half of the park, is a five-dollar bill. Two and a half metres away on the blanket is another one, face-down under a paperback. That is $10.85 — 43% of the goal — inside a four-metre circle.

The three are not the same. picnicker owns the cooler five and the loose change and stands still, facing across the cooler, with a wide cone. frisbee owns the pinned five and paces a three-metre triangle. phone owns nothing, notices nothing, and stands exactly where you want to land.

That third one is doing the most work. A guard you can be seen by is a puzzle; a body you cannot walk through is a constraint on the solution. And the pacing one is what stops the whole thing being solved once — a cone you can memorise is a cone you beat forever, while a cone that walks three metres and comes back makes the same approach safe and unsafe at different moments, without anything on screen moving fast enough to feel unfair.

Asserted, not admired shoot.mjs lifts the cooler five and counts how many non-oblivious people are within five metres at that instant. If a later edit walks somebody away, the check fails with "the set piece has gone soft" rather than the level quietly becoming easy.

The pinned five is level 1's saltshaker in a different hat, and deliberately so. SHOVE is taught on the block, in an empty corner of a café row where nothing is watching. Here the identical two-step happens on a blanket with three people round it, and the extra second it costs is the entire difference.

§5 Defending a silhouette

The roofline learned this the expensive way: the kid you trade with was moved onto a restaurant terrace and became invisible — a slightly shorter adult among a maître d', a busser and two diners. Colour did not fix it and props did not fix it. A silhouette did. She sits down, and nothing else in either block sits down.

A park is where that inheritance stops being free. A park is the one setting that is mostly benches. If the picnic sat on its blanket and the four benches had anybody on them, "the one who is sitting" would identify five people and therefore nobody.

So the park is designed around keeping that silhouette unique. The picnic stands round its cooler — which is also what the set piece needs, because three standing cones cover more ground than three sitting ones. The keeper walks, the jogger runs, the vendor stands. Four benches, all empty. She is still the only person in the game who is sitting down.

Where she sits took two attempts, and both failures were about the fixed camera rather than about her:

  • Far side of the pond. Correct in plan, useless on screen — the pond's own 0.62 m wall stands between a fixed camera and anybody behind it, so she rendered as a tan head above a tan kerb in front of a tan path.
  • Near side, but east. Visible, and directly behind the picnic shelter, which is this block's deliberate near-side occluder. An occluder only fades when it is on the one ray between camera and crow — which is most of the time and not all of it. Trading is core loop. It does not go behind the thing whose job is to be in the way.

She ended up on the near-west arc, 118° round from due east, with the water behind her. A small figure against teal is a small figure you can see.

§6 The pretzel, and one rule the park refuses to teach

Every block's marquee puzzle is the same shape: food dropped far enough from what it guards pulls every bird within reach, and the guard leaves his post to deal with them. Twelve seconds of an unguarded ten.

The roofline adds a second condition and teaches it by failing you — birds do not use stairs, so chips dropped in the yard feed the yard and the terrace carries on exactly as it was. That is a good lesson and it belongs on a block with four decks.

The park declines it. bait.deck is null here, the same as level 1's. There is one deck that matters, the pavilion roof is somewhere to stand rather than somewhere to solve anything, and a level whose job is to be a small step should not smuggle in the next level's rule.

Why a pretzel Because a hot dog is level 1's and a cone of chips is level 3's, and the bait is the one object a player will remember a district by. It is also the cheapest new mesh in the project — a torus at four radial segments, a twist box, and three grains of salt — and adding it turned BAIT_KINDS from a pair into a vocabulary, so the audit's bait rules now cover a fourth kind nobody has written yet.

§7 What the harnesses caught

Every one of these was found by a machine before it was found by a person, which is the entire argument for the two scripts existing.

smoke
A dime in a planter, a dollar inside a noticeboard Rule 6 — no pickup buried in solid geometry. The dollar's ledge collider sat where the board was rather than where the ledge mesh was; the 0.12 m the ledge stands proud is the whole clearance that bill has.
smoke
A lamppost standing in a coin's one sightline Rule 6 again — no pickup hidden from the fixed camera. A 4.6 m column is opaque all the way up, and it was exactly on the ray from the bandstand step's loose change.
smoke
Two patrol waypoints inside solids The keeper walked into the noticeboard and the jogger into a planter, both by less than half a metre, both invisible in the source.
shoot
The lawn was the darkest thing anyone had put on screen Painted in the tree greens, it failed dusk twice over: median 35 against a floor of 48, and the frame stopped being violet, because saturated green swallows the violet sky fill every other block's shadows are made of. One material, both failures.
shoot
…and then the correction over-corrected At a pale sage the dusk numbers were fine and the daylight was a beach: the afternoon key is warm and strong enough to pull an under-saturated green straight to tan, and it took the paving with it. A park with paths in it rendered as one continuous sandpit. Both ends of the ramp, or neither.
shoot
A road darker than the lawn in front of it The roofline's "black river across the foreground", arriving from the other direction. A kerb reads by being a lit edge above a surface, not by being lighter than it — so the road went back up to full paving and took four points of median with it.
shoot
A ninth lamppost that made things worse It bought two points of median and cost four of 5th percentile. At this camera a lamp is a pool on the ground plus a 4.6 m column and its shadow in the frame. Light the ground, not the lamp count — six paths became ten instead.

The dusk frames now sit at p50 52 / p05 27 at the picnic and p50 48 / p05 30 at the cart, against floors of 48 and 24, with blue beating red at both. That is the same band the shipped roofline occupies. The floors were not touched, and must not be.

§8 What it costs the engine

Nothing structural. There is no asset pipeline, no new system, and no new per-frame work.

  • Meshes: 445, against the block's 371 and the roofline's 504.
  • Colliders: 57, against 52 and 62.
  • Night lights: eight lamps and nine ground pools, all emissive or additive quads — no scene lights added, same as both other blocks.
  • New code paths: one, the pretzel case in buildMesh. Everything else is world/kit.js and existing level data.
  • Bundle: unchanged at ~170 kB gzipped, still almost entirely three.js.

The one shared value that moved is the palette: PAL.lawn and PAL.lawnMid are new, and nothing outside this level uses them.

§9 Open questions

  • Is $25 the right ask? The bound that matters is asserted — the honest route reaches $12.85 of $25 — but whether the last ten dollars feel like a grind or like an endgame is a playtest question, and the only lever that is cheap to move is the goal.
  • Is one roof enough of a step? The climb is 1.0 m of unbroken air. A player might reasonably finish the park without ever having thought about altitude, which would leave the roofline exactly as steep a leap as it was.
  • Does the picnic read as dangerous before it is? Three standing figures around a glinting note is legible in a screenshot. Whether it reads as a problem to plan around rather than a coin to walk at is the thing to watch in the first playtest.
  • The jogger. Fast, oblivious, and on the one path every route across the park uses. Either that is good friction or it is a person who keeps bumping into you for no reason, and there is no way to tell from here.
  • How the player gets from block to block. Still not decided, still ?level=N, still deliberately a seam rather than a join.
