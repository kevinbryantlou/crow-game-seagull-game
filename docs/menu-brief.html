§0 Where this stands

Nothing here is built. Every mockup below is HTML and CSS assembled from the game's own tokens — --brass, --enamel, the two typefaces, the real .start and .start.ghost button rules — so what you are looking at is close to what the code would produce, not an impression of it. It is not, however, running.

Built · one playtest round The title card, the pause overlay and the three-button row all read as fine. The round found one bug and one copy fix, both in §8: forgetting your progress did not ask, and it left every surviving screen holding a route back into a block it had just locked.
All five milestones in §11 have landed and every assertion in §10 is in one of the two harnesses — 35 of them in smoke for the save layer and the rest in shoot, which now photographs the menu into shots/15–20. Four things went wrong on the way that this document did not predict; they are recorded at the end of §10 rather than quietly fixed, because three of them are the kind that pass a screenshot.

Revised once against review. Both open decisions are closed and the sections that depended on them have been redrawn rather than annotated: the level list is its own screen (§2), the best run is recorded but shown nowhere (§3), and the pause overlay lost its chip row as a consequence (§4). The rejected argument is kept at the end of §2, because it is the thing this design has to keep answering as blocks are added.

The problem, stated once You finish the block. The ending screen hands you the park. You close the tab. You come back and you are on the block again, with no record that you ever cleared it, and no way to reach the park except by typing ?level=2 into the address bar. The ladder is the spine of this game and the game does not remember climbing it.

§1 What the engine already gives us

Three things fell out of reading main.js, and together they mean this is mostly interface work rather than engine work.

Pause is already free. elapsed only advances inside _tickSim, and _tickSim only runs under if (this.running). Setting running = false stops the simulation and the day clock with it, which is the thing a pause must not get wrong — pausing at 6:40 into an 8-minute day and coming back to find the sun down would be worse than having no pause at all.

The scene keeps rendering while paused. The camera follow, the light ramp and the night lights all sit outside the running guard in _frame. So a pause overlay sits over a living picture — the sun still crawls, the lamps still warm — rather than over a freeze-frame. That is the good version, and it is already the default.

loadLevel(id, autoStart) is the whole menu API. It tears down, rebuilds, drops you straight into play, and keeps the address bar in step. A level chip is a call to it.

The one thing to get right Resuming must reset _last and _acc exactly as begin() does. Otherwise the fixed-step accumulator comes back holding the entire length of the pause and spends it on catch-up steps — the steps < 6 clamp bounds the damage, but the first frame after a pause would still run six ticks of simulation the player did not ask for. input.flush() belongs there too, for the same reason the ending screen needs it: the key that dismissed the overlay is still down.

§3 Decision two: what a chip knows

The chip is the one component the whole feature hangs off: it appears on the title card, in the pause overlay, and — under Option 2 — on the level screen. It has three states and one open question.

The three states

Cleared is full strength with a brass underline. Selected adds a brass outline and nothing else — it may not be brass-filled, because the ending screen's design rule is that there is exactly one loud thing on a card and that thing is the button. A brass chip beside a brass Begin is two. Locked drops to 40% and loses its brass. It stays on screen: the ladder is the reason to keep playing, and a menu that hides what is next hides the reason.

Do not write the rungs down here This paragraph used to name them — "$20 / $25 / $30, one deck / two / four" — and was wrong inside a day, when a fourth block landed at $35 after a playtest moved it down from $40. The chips read goal off the level descriptor, and the registry is the only place the ladder is written. A second copy in prose is a second copy.

The best is recorded, and not shown

Settled, and split: the save keeps your best run per block from day one — most banked, and the clock reading when you hit the goal — and no chip displays it in this iteration. Writing it costs two fields and means the history exists whenever a screen wants it. Displaying it is the part that changes the game rather than the menu, so it waits for a screen designed to carry it.

Why the split, rather than all or nothing A best time on a chip is a design change to the blocks, not to the menu. The sun dial currently means "you are running out of day"; put a clock on the results and it also means "you are being timed", and every block gets replayed by a player optimising against a number rather than robbing a café. Recording it commits to none of that and cannot be done retroactively — a best that starts being written the day it is first displayed shows every returning player an empty history of runs they actually played.

Copy

Chips inherit their names from shortName and their price from goal, both already on the level descriptor and both already used by the ending screen's onward button. A menu that invents a second set of names for the same three blocks is a menu that will disagree with the ending screen within a month.

Locked reads Locked, flatly. It was tempting to write something with more voice in it, but this is wayfinding copy, and the block that taught us that lesson is the roofline — parapet sent an American playtester to a dictionary instead of to a roof edge.

§4 Pause

There is no pause today. It has to exist before a level list is reachable from inside a run, and it is worth having on its own — this is a game with a clock in it, on a phone, where a run gets interrupted by the world.

The overlay is a scrim, not a card. The title and ending screens are opaque #16332a because there is nothing behind them worth looking at. Pause is 78% ink over a scene that is still moving — the sun still crawling, the lamps still warming — because the whole message of a pause screen is your block is still there. That distinction is free, given §1, and losing it would make pause feel like quitting.

The eyebrow carries the block's name and what is in the nest, so the screen answers "where am I and how am I doing" without you having to read the HUD through a scrim. Resume is the loud thing. Start over restarts the current block. Levels opens the screen from §2.

Esc opens and closes it, and the controls legend gains a row — which is also the argument for why touch needs its own affordance, since that legend is display:none under body.touch.

What choosing the separate screen cost here An earlier draft of this overlay carried the chip row directly, so switching blocks was one click from a paused game. With the list on its own card that becomes two, and the pause screen has a third button on it. That is the honest price of a screen that scales past three blocks, and it buys something back: the pause overlay is now short enough to sit comfortably on a phone in landscape, which the chip row was already straining.
Mute is not new M already toggles mute — it is bound in main.js and already listed in the controls legend. The fine print here reminds you of a shortcut that has shipped for months rather than proposing a feature; nothing about audio is being added, and removing it would be taking something away.

§5 Pause, on a phone

There is no Esc on an iPad, and this whole thing is being built to package with Capacitor. A pause the touch build cannot reach is a pause that does not exist on the platform the game is aimed at.

Top-centre is the one piece of chrome that is free: money is top-left, tasks are top-right, the sun dial and the buttons own the bottom-right, the stick appears wherever the left thumb lands. It is also the hardest place on the screen to hit by accident while playing, which matters for a button whose job is to stop the game.

§6 The ending needs a third way out

The ending screen today offers The hotel → and Again!, and that is a complete set of choices for a game with no menu: forward, or repeat. Once a menu exists, an ending that can only go forward or repeat becomes the one screen in the game you cannot get out of.

It does not get a third .start. The card's whole compositional rule is one filled button and one outlined one — a third would flatten the hierarchy the ending was carefully given. It goes in the fine print instead, which is already styled and already sits under the buttons.

§7 Leaving a block you are in the middle of

There is no mid-run save and there should not be one. A block is eight minutes; the session is the unit. Leaving one abandons it.

But it should not abandon it silently when there is something to lose. The rule: if the nest is empty, switching blocks just switches — no friction, nothing was at stake. If you have banked money, the confirm takes over the Levels screen's action row, in place, rather than opening a dialogue on top of it.

This is where the separate screen earns back some of what it cost in §4. Because a chip arms the primary button rather than launching on click, the dangerous action already has a deliberate second press in front of it — the confirm replaces that button's row instead of interrupting a click that was already halfway done.

The copy

Short, and it does not explain the rules of the game back at you. An earlier draft ran to two sentences and spent one of them on nothing about it is kept, which is both obvious from the situation and slightly scolding. What the player needs is the number they are about to lose.

§8 The save file

One new module, core/save.js, pure and DOM-free so smoke.mjs can test it without a browser — the same reason words.js and rank.js are their own files. One key, one versioned blob.

// localStorage["smallchange.progress"]
{
  v: 1,                // bumped when the shape changes; a blob from
                       // another version is discarded, never migrated
  cleared: [1, 2]       // level ids, in no particular order
}

// the larger save from §3, if we take it — same key, same version gate
{
  v: 1,
  cleared: [1, 2],
  best: { 1: { total: 3240, secs: 252 } }   // cents and whole seconds:
}                                            // no floats in a save file

I · The blob. Small on purpose.

It has to be allowed to fail

localStorage is not a guarantee. It throws on access in Safari private mode, it throws on write when the quota is full, and it is one of the things that behaves its own way inside a WKWebView — which is precisely the shell this game is being built to ship in. Every read and every write is wrapped, and a failure degrades to an in-memory store: the menu still works for the session, and nothing survives the tab. The game must be fully playable with no storage at all, and that is a test rather than a hope.

A save must not be trusted

It is a string on a stranger's machine. It can be hand-edited, it can be truncated, and it can have been written by a future build that had five levels in it. So: parse in a try, check v, drop ids that getLevel does not recognise, and treat anything unparseable as "no progress" rather than as an error. A save file that crashes the title card is worse than no save file.

What still bypasses it

?level=N keeps working and keeps ignoring the lock. It is how shoot.mjs drives the game and how you spot-check a block without playing two others first. The menu gates; the URL does not.

Forget my progress One line of fine print, on the Levels screen. It is needed for testing the empty state, and it is the honest thing to put next to the only feature in this game that writes to somebody's machine.

Forgetting asks, and then it takes you back to the beginning

Both halves came out of the first playtest. It is the only action in the game that destroys something the player cannot get back, so it uses the same confirm the forfeit uses — one component, two callers, destructive choice outlined and the way back in brass.

The second half is the less obvious one. Forgetting can re-lock the block you are standing in, and every screen that survives the reset is then holding a key to it: finish the roofline, open Levels from the ending, forget, press Back, and the ending screen is still sitting there offering Again! on a block that was just closed. Reported from a playtest as exactly that sequence. So a reset ends the run and returns to the title on the first block — a reset that leaves you holding a key to the thing it locked is not a reset.

§9 Scope

In

  • core/save.js — versioned, wrapped, unit tested
  • Cleared ids and a best per block, both written from day one
  • A Levels screen, reached from title, pause and ending
  • Continue on the returning title card
  • Pause: Esc, a touch control, a scrim over the live scene
  • The forfeit confirm, on the Levels screen
  • Forget my progress
  • Harness coverage — see §10

Out

  • Displaying the best anywhere. It is recorded and unread.
  • Mid-run save and resume. A block is one sitting.
  • A stats or records screen
  • A settings screen — mute is a key, already bound, already in the legend
  • Achievements, medals, stars
  • A fourth block. This is the thing that makes a fourth block worth having, not the fourth block.

What the build found that this document did not predict

Four, and three of them would have passed a screenshot — which is the argument for driving the interface rather than photographing it.

  1. Two full-screen cards at the same z-index are decided by document order, and the loser eats every click in silence. #pause is at 45 and the other screens at 40, so opening the level list from a paused game left the scrim on top of it; #ending is later in the document than #levels at equal z-index and does the same from the other side. The frame looked perfect and nothing was clickable. Pause is state now, and only one card is ever up.
  2. Every page in one shoot run shares a localStorage. The sections that win a block to photograph its ending now also clear that block, so the navigation test opened a bare URL, got the roofline, and asserted the block's ending copy. Anything needing a known block pins it with ?level=N.
  3. The title card's strapline was markup. It promised "twenty dollars" whatever was loaded — invisible while the card only ever fronted level 1, wrong the moment Continue could open the hotel on it. The money counter's goal had this exact bug once already, in the same file.
  4. A disabled control makes the obvious test pass while exercising nothing. "Click the locked chip, then press Play" passed because the click is a no-op, so the armed id stayed on the current block and Play correctly resumed it. It asserted a locked chip could not be played while never having armed one.

One thing this document was wrong about and the build corrected: top-centre is not free on a phone. §5 reasoned that money is top-left and tasks top-right, so the middle is spare. On a 390 px screen the task chip reached 3 px into a 44 px pause target — measured, not guessed. The task list yields below 430 px, because it is the only one of the three that is a list and can afford the characters.

§11 Order of work

M1
The save module core/save.js plus its smoke tests, including the throwing-storage and garbage-blob cases. Nothing on screen yet. Landing this alone means the persistence layer is never being debugged at the same time as the interface — the same reason level 1 was rebuilt on the kit before the roofline was written.
M2
The Levels screen, and Continue The chip component and its three states, the screen itself, and the returning title card's two buttons. Clearing a block writes to the save. The empty state is exactly today's card, one brass Begin and no second button. At the end of this the feature is real, if only reachable from the front door.
M3
Pause Esc, the scrim, resume and restart, the accumulator reset and the input flush. No level switching in it yet — pause is worth shipping on its own merits and is easier to trust when it does one thing.
M4
Wiring the Levels screen into a running game The Levels button on the pause overlay, the forfeit confirm, the Levels fine print on the ending. This is the milestone that turns four screens into one loop, and the one where the level-swap traps in §10 get exercised.
M5
The touch control, and the harness The pause button and its relationship with the TEST MODE badge; then the shoot work — swaps re-pointed through the menu, the pause assertions, the new screenshots. Read the PNGs.

§12 What review settled

  1. §2 — a separate Levels screen, against the original recommendation, on the grounds that more blocks are coming and a list built for three has to be rebuilt at six. The returning title card and the seven-slot layout in exhibit C2 were drawn in response and are the thing to look at.
  2. §3 — the best is recorded and displayed nowhere. A split rather than a yes or no: writing it is free and cannot be done retroactively, showing it changes what a block is about.
  3. §5 — the TEST MODE badge pushes the pause control down, never the reverse. Confirmed.
  4. §6 — the ending gets its Levels route. Confirmed.
  5. §7 — the forfeit copy is one line, not three: Are you sure? You already collected $12.60.

One thing raised that turned out not to be a change

Mute read as a new feature in the pause fine print. It is not — M has been bound in main.js for months and is already listed in the controls legend. Nothing about audio is proposed here; the overlay only names a shortcut that already exists. Worth a second look only if the fine print should be quieter.

Still open: the game already has a word for this, and it is not "level"

The Levels screen introduces level to player-facing copy. That was raised here as a hypothetical and checked, and it is not hypothetical — block is already on screen in four places, one of which every single player reads before they read anything else:

So this is a real collision rather than a style preference, and the last of those is the one that settles it: Not in blocks. In wingbeats. is the closing line of the whole first level and the word is load-bearing — it is a unit of city, which is the entire joke. That line cannot become "levels" and should not.

Which points at the resolution: a block is the place, a level is the slot. Fiction says block, furniture says level. The screen is called Levels, the chips are numbered 01–03, and the prose never stops saying block. That is coherent as long as it is decided on purpose — it is being flagged rather than fixed because it is one line in a brief now and a search-and-replace later.
